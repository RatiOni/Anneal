You already know you got less done than you meant to. You probably can’t say how much less.

Habitica, Finch and the rest ask you to tick a box, then congratulate you for ticking it. You can tick a box in half a second from bed. This one runs a timer, so the only way to move the number is to sit there.

Once a week it tells you one thing

Once a week it lines up what you said you’d do against what the timer actually recorded, then gives you one thing to change. Only one. You’d ignore a list of nine.

Declared against completed
64percent completed
Stop starting sessions after 21:00. Every one you abandoned this week began after that hour.

Six things it won’t do

Punish you for a bad week Skip a week and the forge goes dark. Nothing gets deleted. Come back in March and every mark you earned is still on the rack.
Assume every day looks the same Commitments are counted by the week. Four sessions in seven days, whichever seven days those are. Night shifts and flu don’t break anything.
Hand you eleven systems on day one Week one is a timer and nothing else. Habitica opens with dailies, habits, to-dos, gold, health and a party, which is why people bounce off it.
Let you game your own score The number is minutes on a clock. You can pad a to-do list with “drink water”. You can’t pad a timer.
Make you tend a rotting list Anything you stop touching files itself away. You won’t come back after two months off and face forty stale items.
Nag you There’s no streak counter, no notifications, and no email three weeks later asking whether you’ve given up on yourself.

Your record never leaves your machine

There’s no account and no server. Your history is a SQLite file in your AppData folder. Open it, copy it to a USB stick, delete it. If I stop working on this, you still have the file.

Syncing between machines is the one part that costs me money every month. That’s the part I charge for.
